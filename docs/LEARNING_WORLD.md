## 1. The core loop: the Lineside Loop

Every topic runs the same five steps, and each step happens somewhere you can walk to.

| Step | Plain name | In the world | What happens | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SEE** | **The Board** (a chalkboard or whiteboard inside the host building) | A host explains the idea in 5 to 7 short visual beats, then hands you a live **explorable**: a toy version of the concept you can poke. | 8 to 12 min |
| 2 | **TRY** | **The Bench** (a workbench or table in the same room) | Three puzzles that get harder, with instant feedback that explains why. You rate your confidence before each one is marked. | 8 to 12 min |
| 3 | **TALK** | **The Brew** (a tea corner, snug or mess room) | Two or three classmates with different views, chaired by the host. They ask, disagree, and ask you to settle it. You teach it back to Dev. | 6 to 10 min |
| 4 | **DO** | **Out on the line** (a live situation somewhere in Harrowby) | You're dropped into the middle of something that's already going on. Find out where you are, work out what matters, decide, and tell people. The host is on the phone as your coach. | 12 to 20 min |
| 5 | **LOOK BACK** | **The Logbook** (Moira's kitchen table at Beck Cottage, or a pop-up card) | Your choices next to an expert's, how your confidence compared with your results, your own one-line takeaway, a lamp lit. | 3 to 5 min |

The tagline for the website: **"Board, Bench, Brew, then out on the line."**

### What makes it different (the six signature mechanics)

1. **Knowledge becomes a tool you carry.** Each topic you finish gives you a **Lens**, a way of seeing the world. Once you've done Planning, the Planning Lens (hold Q, or tap the lens button on a phone) draws glowing dependency threads between jobs in the diorama and lights the critical chain in lamp amber. The project office board, which is set dressing today, becomes a working plan you can inspect. You don't just score what you've learned; you can see with it.
2. **Mastery is lit, not listed.** Harrowby's old railway lamps line the route from the station to Beck Cottage, one for each topic. Finish a topic and its lamp lights. Gold mastery gives it a brass trim. It suits the HD-2D lighting: as you learn, the valley gets visibly brighter at dusk.
3. **Lamps dim, and relighting them is spaced retrieval.** After 1, 3, 7 and 21 days a lamp starts to flicker. A townsperson stops you with a 60-second "lamp check": one quick question on that concept, set in their own life. June asks whether a late oven makes the rolls late. Answer it and the lamp relights. It's spaced retrieval practice, and it feels like village life rather than a test.
4. **You pick how you learn at the door.** At each Board the host asks: "Show me, tell me, or let me have a go first?" *Let me have a go* sends you straight to the explorable and the first puzzle, then back to the explanation (productive failure, which suits professionals). *Show me* runs the visual beats first. *Tell me* adds a spoken, story-led version. A second choice, "new to this" or "I do this for a living", decides whether you're offered a **test-out**: pass Puzzle 3 first time and you go straight to the Brew.
5. **Teach-back to Dev.** Dev Mistry (17, wants to study engineering) turns up at every Brew and asks you to explain the idea "like I'm new". Explaining it is the strongest evidence of understanding we can collect. It's marked against the key points on the concept card.
6. **Apply is a situation, not a quiz.** You arrive part-way through, with incomplete information and a clock that moves when you act, not in real time, so it's calm and accessible. The first skill tested is "where am I and what matters?", which is what real delivery work feels like on a Saturday morning.

## 3. Module 1: Project Planning (full specification)

**Host:** Jo Adeyemi. **Room:** the back room of the project office, with a whiteboard (the Board), a big table covered in sticky notes (the Bench) and a kettle on a filing cabinet (the Brew). **Total:** about 45 minutes, and it can be split at any step. Progress saves at each step.

### 3.1 Learning outcomes

- **P1.** Break a job into activities and link them by what has to finish first (finish-to-start dependencies).
- **P2.** Find the critical path and the finish time of a small network, and work out float.
- **P3.** Predict what a delay or a speed-up does to the finish, including when the critical path moves.
- **P4.** Plan against a fixed end, such as a possession handback or a road re-opening, and keep a margin.
- **P5.** In a live situation, find the real critical problem among louder distractions, re-plan safely and tell people clearly.

### 3.2 SEE: the Board (explainer beats)

Each beat is 20 to 40 seconds: one idea, one picture, Jo's voice line and a "Next" button. The example is Jo making a round of tea for the crew, which ties in to the *Kettle's On* achievement.

| Beat | On screen | Jo says (roughly) | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1. **A plan is an order, not a list of dates** | Six sticky notes pop onto the board: *Boil kettle 3 min · Fetch mugs 1 · Teabags in 0.5 · Pour 0.5 · Brew 3 · Milk and serve 0.5* | "Six jobs. What's the first thing you'd do? Wrong answers welcome." | Activities and durations |
| B2. **Some jobs wait for others** | Arrows draw themselves: mugs → teabags; kettle and teabags → pour → brew → milk | "You can't pour before it's boiled. That's a dependency: this one finishes, then that one starts." | Finish-to-start logic |
| B3. **Jobs can run side by side** | The notes slide onto a timeline. The mugs lane runs alongside the kettle. | "While the kettle boils, you fetch the mugs. That's free time, and a good plan spots it." | Parallel paths |
| B4. **The longest chain sets the finish** | Two chains are traced in chalk: kettle chain 7 min, mugs chain 1.5 min then a wait. The kettle chain lights amber and a flag reads "7 min". | "Follow every chain start to finish. The longest one is the **critical path**. Delay anything on it and the tea's late." | Critical path |
| B5. **Float** | A grey ghost bar appears after "teabags in": 1.5 min | "The mugs can be 1.5 minutes late and nobody notices. That's **float**. It belongs to the plan, not to whoever fetches the mugs." | Float, and who owns it |
| B6. **On the railway, the finish is often a wall** | Zoom out to a weekend on a calendar. A red wall reads "HANDBACK 06:00 MON". | "On a railway job you don't finish when you finish. You finish before the line's handed back, and the gap before that wall is your margin." | Fixed ends, margin |
| B7. **The critical path can move** | Someone's washing the mugs: mugs goes up to 3. The chains swap colour. | "Change one job and a different chain can become the longest. Keep looking." | Criticality shifts |

### 3.3 SEE: the explorable (Jo's Planning Table)

A live network and timeline, drawn on the table in the diorama. On desktop it's mouse and keyboard; on a phone it's taps only, with no fine dragging needed.

**Interactions**

| Action | Desktop | Phone | What the learner sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add an activity | Drag a card from the tray | Tap the card, then tap "Place" | The card drops onto the timeline at its earliest start |
| Link two jobs | Drag from a card's right tab to another card's left tab | Tap card A, then card B | A chalk arrow. B slides right if it has to wait. |
| Remove a link | Click the arrow, press Delete | Tap the arrow, then the bin | Things slide back |
| Loop protection | — | — | If a link would create a loop, the card wobbles and Jo says: "That'd make the kettle wait for itself." |
| Change a duration | Drag the bar's right edge, or use the arrow keys | Buttons from −1 to +1 | Everything after it ripples in real time over 150 ms |
| Show float | Toggle | Toggle | Grey ghost bars show how far each card can slip |
| Show early and late times | Toggle | Toggle | ES/EF/LS/LF appear in small print on each card (professional view) |
| Set a finish wall | Drag the red wall | Stepper | Margin is shown in green. Anything past the wall shows negative float in red. |
| Tidy | Button | Button | Auto-layout by earliest start |

The critical chain always glows amber, like the lamps, and a small flag shows the finish time. The engine recalculates earliest and latest times with a forward and a backward pass on every change. With fewer than 20 nodes that's instant.

**Built-in challenges** (each ticks off when done; hints come from Jo):

1. **"Make the mugs critical."** Add at least 1.5 min to the mugs chain. *Teaches:* float runs out, and then the path changes.
2. **"Tea in under 6 minutes."** The only way is to swap *Boil kettle* for *Use the hall urn* (0 min, "on since 1987"). The finish drops to 5.5 min and the mugs chain becomes critical. Trying to shorten *Brew* is locked, with the line "Nobody's having weak tea on my watch." *Teaches:* shorten the critical path and another path takes over; some durations are fixed by physics or quality, like brewing, curing or cooling.
3. **"Add the milk run."** A new card, *Fetch milk from the shop, 5 min*, is needed before *Milk and serve*. It has 1.5 min of float. Make it 7 min and it becomes critical, finishing at 7.5. *Teaches:* one late delivery only matters if it's on, or runs out of float onto, the critical path.
4. **"Load the real job"** (the bridge to TRY). The same table loads the Crag Lane drain job below.

### 3.4 TRY: the Bench (three puzzles)

The same rules apply to each puzzle. You rate your confidence first (Unsure, Fairly sure or Certain), which is marked for calibration the way the Calls are today. You get two tries. Feedback comes after every answer and explains why, and it's worded for the specific mistake. Hints are free but recorded.

#### Puzzle 1: "Find the critical path" (P2), the Crag Lane cess drain

*Tom's crew are renewing 60 metres of blocked cess drain near Crag Lane. Here's the job.*

| Code | Activity | Days | Must wait for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Survey and mark out | 2 | — |
| B | Order and deliver new pipes | 5 | A |
| C | Clear vegetation (ecologist present) | 3 | A |
| D | Dig the trench | 4 | C |
| E | Lay and joint the pipes | 3 | B and D |
| F | Backfill and reinstate the cess | 2 | E |

**Task:** tap the activities on the critical path, then give the finish day and the float on B.
**Answer:** A → C → D → E → F. Finish at the end of **day 14**. **B has 2 days of float** (B finishes day 7; E can't start until D finishes on day 9).

| If the learner… | Feedback |
|---|---|
| Is correct | "Spot on. A, C, D, E, F: 14 days. The pipes are the biggest single job, but they're not the longest chain. They've got two days in hand." |
| Picks A-B-E-F (misconception **M1**: the longest task is critical) | "B is the longest single job, but critical is about the longest *chain*. Add up A-C-D: that's 9 days before E can start. A-B is only 7." |
| Gives the finish as 19 (added everything) (**M5**) | "That's every job end to end, as if one person did them all. C and D can run while the pipes are on order." |
| Gets the path right but float wrong | "Nearly. B finishes on day 7 and E starts on day 9. The gap is B's float." |

#### Puzzle 2: "The weekend possession" (P1, P4)

*A possession of the branch runs from **22:00 Friday** to handback at **06:00 Monday** (56 hours). Nine jobs renew a length of track. Put them in order on the table (some can run side by side), then say when you'd be ready to hand back.*

| # | Activity | Hours | Clue shown on the card |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take the possession and set up the worksite | 2 | "Nothing starts until the site's set up and protected." |
| 2 | Remove the old track | 6 | — |
| 3 | Dig out old ballast, lay new drainage | 10 | "Needs the old track out." |
| 4 | Drop bottom ballast from the engineering train | 4 | "Goes on the new formation." |
| 5 | Lay new sleepers and rail | 12 | "Needs bottom ballast down." |
| 6 | Top ballast and first tamp | 8 | "Needs the rails in." |
| 7 | Welding rail joints | 6 | "Needs the rails in. Can run alongside the ballasting on a different length." |
| 8 | Second tamp and check track geometry | 4 | "Needs both 6 and 7." |
| 9 | Final inspection and hand back | 2 | "Last." |

**Answers:**
- Order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → (6 in parallel with 7) → 8 → 9.
- Earliest handback **22:00 Sunday** (48 h), which leaves **8 hours of margin** before 06:00 Monday.
- Welding has **2 h of float**.
- Follow-up question: "The tamper develops a fault and the first tamp takes 13 hours instead of 8. Can you still hand back on time?" Yes, at **03:00 Monday**, with 3 hours to spare. Welding now has 7 h of float.

| If the learner… | Feedback |
|---|---|
| Puts 7 after 6 in series | "Works, but you've spent 6 hours you had for free. Welding and ballasting happen on different lengths, so run them together." |
| Says a 5-hour fault costs 5 hours of margin but welding is unaffected | Correct. Jo adds: "And notice the margin went from 8 to 3. Margin is for faults like this. Don't budget it away on Friday." |
| Suggests "hand back late, it's only a bit" (**M6**) | "On a working railway a late handback delays trains and passengers, and it's treated as a serious failure. The handback time is a wall. The plan has to fit inside it, with room to spare." |

*Subject-matter check (rail PM sign-off):* the durations are illustrative. On a real job, stressing may happen in a later shift; if so, card 7's clue says so.

#### Puzzle 3: "What slips if…" (P3, and the professional test-out)

Back to the Puzzle 1 drain job. Each question starts from the original plan (finish day 14).

| Q | Change | Options | Answer | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | The pipes arrive 2 days late (B: 5 → 7) | 14 / 16 / 12 | **14** | "No change: B had exactly two days of float. But now there are two critical paths, and you've no slack left on the pipes." |
| b | Clearing takes an extra day (C: 3 → 4) | 14 / **15** / 17 | **15** | "C is on the critical path, so every day there is a day at the end." |
| c | Tom adds a second gang and the trench takes 2 days (D: 4 → 2) | **12** / 10 / 14 | **12** | "Two days saved, because D was critical. But look: A-B-E-F is 12 as well now." |
| d | Tom offers four gangs to dig in 1 day (D: 4 → 1) | 11 / **12** / 13 | **12** | "No better than two gangs. The pipes are now the long chain (A-B is 7 days). And four gangs in one trench is a crowd, not a plan." (**M3**: adding people always shortens it) |

Scoring: 4 correct = full marks; 3 = passed. Passing Puzzle 3 first time in "I do this for a living" mode counts as a test-out.

#### The concept card (what every AI line is grounded on)

```
CONCEPT: Project planning and the critical path (Module 1)
KEY IDEAS (the only facts the characters may state):
 K1 An activity is a piece of work with a duration and a clear finish.
 K2 A dependency means one activity must finish before another starts (finish-to-start). Others exist; beginners use this one.
 K3 A path is any chain of dependent activities from start to finish.
 K4 The critical path is the longest path by total duration. It sets the earliest finish.
 K5 Float is how far an activity can slip without moving the finish. Critical activities have zero float.
 K6 Float belongs to the project, not to whoever owns an activity. Using it up makes the path critical.
 K7 Shortening a critical activity can shorten the finish, but only until another path becomes the longest.
 K8 Adding people helps only critical work, only if the work can be split, and only up to a limit (space, safety, supervision).
 K9 Railway work often has a fixed end (the handback or a road reopening). Plans need a margin before it, and a checkpoint to decide whether to carry on or fall back.
 K10 The critical path can change as work progresses. Replan from the actual status, not the original plan.
WORKED EXAMPLES: tea round (7 min, float 1.5); drain job (14 days, B float 2); possession (48 h, margin 8 h).
OUT OF SCOPE: resource levelling algorithms, lag/lead types, software brands, contract clauses.
NEVER: name real organisations or tools; state a rule, standard or number not on this card; judge a safety answer.
```

#### Ten seed prompts (the model varies the wording; the meaning stays fixed)

1. (Jo) "In the drain job, B was the longest job but not critical. Why is 'longest job' such a tempting mistake?"
2. (Pat) "I never needed arrows. I knew what came next. Is drawing the logic worth the time on a small job?"
3. (Amira) "The pipes have two days of float. Whose two days are they: the supplier's, Tom's or the project's?"
4. (Tom) "A plan with zero margin before the handback. Would you start that possession? What would you want first?"
5. (Jo) "Name something in your own life with a critical path." (a warm-up for beginners)
6. (Amira) "If we crash the critical path by two days, what should we check straight away?"
7. (Pat) "In '94 the tamper broke at 02:00. Where would you look in the plan first, and why?"
8. (Tom) "Four gangs in one trench. What goes wrong that the plan doesn't show?"
9. (Jo) "When should you stop following the plan and re-plan?"
10. (Dev) "Can you explain critical path to me like I've never seen a plan?" (teach-back, always the last prompt)

### 3.6 DO: *Hour Eight* (the Apply scenario)

**Saturday, 06:10. Hour eight of 56.** You're taking over from Tom, who has run the night shift for the Crag Lane level crossing deck and culvert renewal. He goes off at 06:30 to rest (the working-hours limits apply to everyone). The road through the crossing is closed under a council permit until **06:00 Monday**, when the school buses need it. At **07:00** Helen and the council's highways officer expect a status call.

You arrive at the site cabin by the crossing with a mug of tea and very little information.

**The baseline plan** (on the cabin board; hours from 22:00 Friday):

| # | Activity | Planned | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Set up the worksite and road closure | 0–2 | 2 |
| S2 | Remove the crossing deck and track | 2–6 | 4 |
| S3 | Dig out to the culvert | 6–12 | 6 |
| S4 | Lift out the old culvert, place new precast units (crane hired 10:00–20:00 Sat = hours 12–22) | 12–20 | 8 |
| S5 | Backfill and compact | 20–24 | 4 |
| S6 | Bottom ballast (needs the ballast delivery) | 24–28 | 4 |
| S7 | Relay the track | 28–38 | 10 |
| S8 | Top ballast and tamp | 38–44 | 6 |
| S9 | New crossing deck and road surface | 44–50 | 6 |
| S10 | Test crossing lights and barriers, inspect, reopen the road | 50–53 | 3 |

Planned margin: **3 h** (53 against 56).

**Evidence in the world.** Each action moves the clock on. There are 50 minutes until the call and nearly an hour of possible looking, so choosing what to check is part of the test.

| Where | Who or what | Clock | What you learn | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Site cabin | Tom's handover (only before 06:30) | 10 min | "Dig-out's behind. We found an old clay pipe nobody drew. Needs cutting out and capping: three hours extra, I reckon." | **Critical** |
| Site cabin | Handover sheet (after 06:30, if you missed Tom) | 5 min | "Unrecorded pipe at culvert. Extra time TBC." (vaguer) | Critical, less clear |
| The dig | Walk down and look (Planning Lens shows S3 lit amber) | 10 min | The trench is 40% dug; the pipe is visible; the gang is waiting on a cutter | Confirms the +3 h |
| Phone | Steve: ballast delivery | 5 min | "Ballast train's lost its path. New arrival 14:00 instead of 10:00." (loud, everyone's talking about it) | **Distractor**: needed at hour 24, arrives at hour 16 |
| Compound | Crane hire sheet (Dale Lifting, fictional) | 5 min | Hire ends 20:00; extension £1,800 if booked before 09:00 | The crane window |
| Crossing barrier | Council permit | 5 min | "Road closed until 06:00 Monday. Extension only by prior agreement." | The wall |
| Compound | Gaz and the crossing deck panels | 5 min | "Panels and kerbs are all here. I could pre-assemble them on the hardstanding today if you give me two fitters." | Opportunity (fast-tracking) |
| Welfare cabin | Hannah | 5 min | A second gang is available from 10:00 and needs a briefing. Nobody extends a shift past its limit. | Safety frame |
| Crossing | Jess (dog walk) | 5 min | "The 07:30 school bus on Monday: will it get through?" | Stakeholder |
| Anywhere | Weather board | 2 min | Showers Sunday afternoon | Minor |

**What the learner has to work out** (with the Planning Table, which opens on the cabin board):
- With S3 +3 h the new finish is **hour 56**, **zero margin**, and S4 now runs hours 15–23, **one hour past the crane window**. There are two problems, not one.
- The late ballast is **not** critical (8 h of float remains).

**Decisions** (choose any, then confirm; each has a cost and an effect):

| Id | Action | Cost | Effect on the plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Brief the second gang for 10:00 and dig from the other side | £3,200 | S3 +1.5 h instead of +3 h. S4 then fits the crane window (ends hour 21.5). |
| D2 | Extend the crane hire by 2 h | £1,800 | Fixes the crane window only |
| D3 | Gaz pre-assembles the deck panels in the compound | £900 | S9 on the critical path: 6 h → 3 h |
| D4 | Spend the morning chasing the ballast train | Your time | No effect on the finish (the trap) |
| D5 | Cut the crossing tests to 1 hour | — | **Refused by Hannah**. Graded poor on safety. Authored line only. |
| D6 | Give the council early warning now: at risk, low margin, next update at a set time | Free | No time saved; builds trust; a fallback conversation starts early |
| D7 | Set a checkpoint: if the relay isn't finished by hour 42 (16:00 Sunday), start the fallback (temporary road surface; track completed in a later possession) | Free | Protects the wall. Professional-level credit. |
| D8 | Ask Tom's gang to stay on through the morning | — | **Poor**: fatigue. Hannah steps in. |

**Outcomes the engine calculates:**

| Package | Margin | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| D1 + D3 (+ D6, D7) | **4.5 h** | Best: cheapest route to a healthy margin |
| D2 + D3 | 3.0 h | Good, but costs more and leaves the dig-out slow |
| D1 alone | 1.5 h | Workable only with D7 |
| D1 + D2 + D3 | 4.5 h | Good, but the crane extension was spent for nothing (a cost note) |
| D2 alone, or nothing | 0 h | Fails: no margin before the wall |
| D4 as the first action | — | The diagnosis is marked down: the loud problem isn't always the critical one |

**The 07:00 call.** You build a status update from sentence chips, so it works on a phone. Free text is optional.
> *Status:* [On plan / At risk / Late] · *Why:* [unrecorded pipe at culvert +3 h / ballast train late / crane] · *What we're doing:* [chips for the chosen actions] · *Margin now:* [x h] · *Next update / decision point:* [time] · *What I need from you:* [chips]

The expert answer: "At risk. An unrecorded pipe at the culvert has added about 3 hours to the dig-out. We're bringing in a second gang at 10:00 and pre-assembling the deck panels, which gets us back to about 4½ hours in hand. The ballast train is late but it isn't on the critical path. Next update at 18:00. If the relay isn't finished by 16:00 tomorrow we move to the temporary road surface so the buses get through on Monday."

**Success criteria and rubric** (100 points, deterministic, never AI-marked):

| Dimension | Points | Full marks when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Find out** | 20 | Heard Tom before 06:30 *or* went to the dig; checked the crane sheet and the permit |
| **Diagnose** | 25 | Named S3 as the critical problem and gave the new finish (hour 56 / zero margin) and the crane clash; did not name the ballast as critical |
| **Decide** | 25 | Margin restored to at least 2 h; no unsafe option chosen; cost reasonable |
| **Tell** | 20 | Status update includes cause, action, margin, next update and the fallback |
| **Control** | 10 | Set a checkpoint (D7) or an equivalent trigger |

Choosing D5 or D8 caps the scenario at "Not yet", whatever the rest of the score. Safety is a gate, not a trade-off. Bands: **85+ Gold lamp · 65–84 Silver · 45–64 Bronze · under 45 Not yet** (replay offered with a fresh variation: a different surprise pipe, supplier or window).

**The coach (Jo on the phone).** There's a phone button with a label. Each call climbs a hint ladder, and the calls are counted as **independence**, not marked as failure.
1. **Question:** "What's everyone waiting on right now?"
2. **Pointer:** "Have you been down to the dig yet?" or "When does the crane go home?"
3. **Partial structure:** the cabin board highlights S3 and S4 and asks you to put the new times in.

Jo never says which decision to take. If you ask "Should I cut the tests?", the answer is always Hannah's authored line.

### 3.7 LOOK BACK: the Logbook

At Beck Cottage (or as a card on the spot):
- **Your timeline against the expert's:** what you checked, in what order, when you found the pipe, and what you chose.
- **Calibration:** your confidence ratings across the five checkpoints against your results. "You were certain three times and right twice."
- **Luck and judgement:** a random variation (for example, the cutter arriving early) is shown as luck and never changes the grade, as in Chapter 1.
- **Your takeaway line** from the Brew, and one sentence from Moira ("You went to the dig. Most people ring someone first.").
- **In the world:** the Planning lamp lights; the Planning Lens unlocks; the project office board becomes live; the first lamp check is booked.

**Lamp checks for Planning** (examples, 60 seconds each):
- June: "If the oven's 10 minutes late warming, are the rolls late, or just the scones?" (float)
- Len: "When we had one train on the branch, did the fireman's tea break hold the train up?" (critical or not)

## 4. The AI: its job and its limits

### The rule: the engine decides, the model speaks

All teaching decisions (the next move, grading, unlocks, safety answers) are deterministic and authored. The small in-browser model is a **voice and a listener**: it words what the engine has chosen, and it helps sort free-text answers into categories. This keeps the principle already in `docs/DESIGN.md`: decisions are graded consistently, and nothing unsafe is invented.

### Three tiers, so it works everywhere

| Tier | What | Size | Where it runs | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0: Authored** (always on) | Scripted branching: every move has 3 to 5 authored variants per persona; answers are chips or multiple choice | 0 | Everywhere, including `file://` | The full experience with no model at all |
| **1: Listener** | A small sentence-embedding model plus nearest-neighbour matching against labelled example answers for each misconception | ~25–45 MB | Phones and school laptops (WASM) | Classifying free-text answers; checking teach-back points |
| **2: Voice** | A 1–3B-parameter, open-licence instruct model, quantised to 4-bit | ~0.8–2 GB, cached after the first download | Desktops and laptops with WebGPU | Varied persona dialogue, Socratic follow-ups, paraphrased feedback, new wordings of the seed prompts |

On first launch the game offers: "Download the classroom voices? (about 1 GB, once)". The authored tier plays while it downloads. If the device can't run Tier 2, it drops to Tier 1 or 0 without comment. None of the learner's text leaves the device.

### What the model must do

- Speak as a persona, from a **persona card** (voice, stance, catchphrases, banned phrases) plus the **concept card**.
- Carry out a chosen move: `probe`, `affirm_extend`, `counter(Mx)`, `debate(K)`, `scaffold`, `redirect` or `teachback_ask`.
- Produce varied wordings of the seed prompts (the meaning is fixed by prompt id).
- Paraphrase authored feedback for the learner's own wording ("You said 'biggest job'…").
- Classify (Tier 1 first, Tier 2 as a tie-breaker): return `{label, confidence, key_points_hit[]}` from a fixed list.

### What it must never do

- State a fact, number, rule or standard that isn't on the concept card.
- Grade safety-critical answers, or answer any safety question. Those always get authored lines from Hannah or Tom.
- Give the Apply answer (the coach has a hint ladder, not a solution).
- Name real organisations, suppliers, operators, regulators, tools or brands. Use the one first name banned in `AGENTS.md`. Be sarcastic, dark or rude.
- Change a score. Classification only chooses the next move and flags; grades come from rubrics.

### How it's grounded and guarded

1. **Input contract** (per turn): `{persona_card, concept_card, move, target (K# or M#), learner_answer, last_3_turns, level, style}`.
2. **Output contract:** JSON `{say: string ≤ 60 words, ask: string|null}`, with constrained decoding where the runtime supports it.
3. **Anchors:** some moves have required phrases (Tom's wall line, Hannah's safety lines), which are always inserted verbatim.
4. **Post-filter:** a banned-terms list (real organisations and brands, the banned first name, rude words); a check for numbers not on the card; length. If any check fails, the authored variant is used instead, silently.
5. **Confidence gate:** if classification confidence is under 0.6, the engine asks a clarifying chip question ("Did you mean the longest job or the longest chain?") rather than guessing.
6. **Evaluation before release:** a golden set of about 200 labelled learner answers per module (from playtests). Tier 1 must reach at least 85% agreement with the learning designer's labels, and Tier 2 outputs are spot-checked (100 samples, zero fact or brand violations) before a module's AI is switched on.

## 5. The business: "Come and do a scenario for me"

**The pitch:** a training scenario that feels like a Saturday on site, built from *your* way of working, in six weeks.

### What the company gives us

- **Their process:** planning procedure, gate stages, safety and possession planning rules in outline, and their templates.
- **One typical job** they want people better at (for example, a weekend bridge deck replacement or a station canopy renewal), with a real programme, anonymised.
- **Three pain points** (for example, late handovers, optimistic durations, poor early warning) and one lesson-learned story.
- **Their vocabulary** list, and **4 × 1 hour of expert time** (kick-off, logic review, playtest, sign-off).

### What we deliver

| Package | Contents | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Scenario** | One new Apply scenario (like *Hour Eight*) on an existing module, with rubric, coach ladder and report | 2–3 weeks |
| **Module** | A full Lineside Loop: concept card, Board beats, explorable config, 3 puzzles, 3 personas, 1 scenario, lamp checks | 6 weeks |
| **Programme** | A chapter of 3–4 modules on their lifecycle, plus a facilitator guide for blended sessions | 12–16 weeks |

Every client scenario is **fictionalised** into Harrowby or a sister location (a coastal town, an urban viaduct, a freight yard). That protects their confidentiality and keeps to our no-real-names rule. Their process and pain points drive the logic; their name never appears.

### Why new modules are cheap

The engine, world, art kit, AI tiers and report are shared. A module is **data**. Puzzles use a small set of templates: `network` (critical path), `sequence`, `what_if`, `choose`, `allocate` (budget or people), `classify` (risk or stakeholder sorting) and `estimate_range`. Explorables use a matching set: `network_table`, `risk_matrix`, `stakeholder_map`, `ev_chart`, `range_slider`, `scope_fence`.

## 6. Assessment and evidence (for L&D buyers)

### Learning outcomes per module

| Module | Outcomes (learners will be able to…) |
|---|---|
| 0 Scope | Define a project's objective, deliverables and boundary; spot and route scope creep |
| 1 Planning | P1–P5 (see §3.1) |
| 2 Estimating | Estimate as a range with stated assumptions; test an estimate against similar past jobs; spot optimism bias |
| 3 Risk and opportunity | Describe a risk as cause, event and effect; rate and prioritise it; choose a proportionate response and owner; spot opportunities |
| 4 Stakeholders | Map interest and influence; tailor a message; communicate uncertainty honestly |
| 5 Safety and assurance | Explain why railway work is planned, briefed and protected; recognise when to stop; describe the purpose of permits, possessions and hold points in general terms |
| 6 Change control | Record, assess (time, cost, risk) and decide a change before acting on it |
| 7 Cost and EV | Compare planned, earned and actual value; read schedule and cost variance; forecast the outturn simply |
| 8 Leading people | Brief a crew clearly; balance pace with fatigue and morale; give and receive feedback |

### What's measured (every item is tagged with a module outcome id)

| Measure | Source |
|---|---|
| **First-attempt accuracy** | Puzzles |
| **Misconceptions found and cleared** | Brew classifier and puzzle feedback |
| **Teach-back coverage** | Key points hit (Tier 1, or learner self-check against a checklist in Tier 0) |
| **Scenario rubric** (Find out, Diagnose, Decide, Tell, Control) | Apply |
| **Safety gates** | Apply |
| **Calibration** | Confidence ratings against results |
| **Independence** | Coach calls and hints |
| **Retention** | Lamp checks at 1, 3, 7 and 21 days |
| **Self-rated confidence, before and after** | The existing `selfCheck` pattern, extended per module |

### The report

- **Learner view (the Logbook):** lamps, lenses, takeaway lines, a calibration chart, and a printable certificate per module with the scenario band.
- **Manager or cohort view (export):** a one-page PDF per learner and a CSV per cohort, with per-outcome mastery (Not yet, Bronze, Silver, Gold), scenario dimension scores, safety flags, the three most common misconceptions in the cohort, retention at 21 days, and the change in confidence from before to after.
- **LMS integration:** a SCORM 1.2 wrapper for completion and score, and xAPI statements (`experienced`, `attempted`, `answered`, `mastered`, `retained`) with outcome ids for richer learning record stores.
- **Privacy:** everything stays on the device by default. Exports hold scores and chip choices only; free-text answers are included only if the learner chooses to share them.
- **Facilitated use:** a facilitator guide per module turns the Brew prompts into a 30-minute group session. The Apply scenario can be played on one screen by a table of four, with a vote at each decision.
